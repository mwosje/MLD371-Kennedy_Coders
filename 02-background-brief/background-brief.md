# City of Plymouth, MN — Background Brief

## 1. What the organization does

The City of Plymouth is a municipal government serving about 79,000 residents in suburban Hennepin County, roughly 12 miles west of Minneapolis. Its mission is “We All Work Together to Add Quality to Life.” Its guiding values are customer focus, integrity, being visionary, inclusion, and connections.

Major services include police and fire protection; streets and infrastructure; water and sewer; parks and recreation; planning and zoning; housing and economic development; building permits and inspections; transit; recycling; elections; finance; communications; IT; and other administrative services.

The organization serves residents, businesses, property owners, developers, visitors, and others who live or work in Plymouth.

## 2. Who runs it

Plymouth uses a council-manager form of government. The seven-member City Council, consisting of one mayor and six council members, sets policy and the city's overall direction. The City Manager, Dave Callister, is appointed by the Council and serves as the chief administrative officer responsible for day-to-day operations and city departments.

According to the team's supplied information, as of September 2026, Jeff Wosje is mayor and Maria Solano is deputy city manager.

The city reports more than 320 full-time employees, roughly 300 temporary or seasonal employees, 300 election judges, and 30 part-time or on-call firefighters. The city's 2026 General Fund expenditures are about $58.6 million. This figure covers the General Fund only; it excludes other capital, utility, and fund spending.

The Council establishes policy, priorities, and budgets, while the city manager and department heads administer them. Current strategic priorities listed in the supplied information are:

- Operational excellence
- City Center reimagined
- A connected and thriving community
- Environmental stewardship
- Economic stability and vitality

## 3. What the work looks like day to day

Work varies considerably by department. At the senior-management level, three recurring activities appear especially relevant:

- **Coordinating departments and resolving issues.** The city manager oversees departments including Public Works, Public Safety, Parks, Finance, and Community & Economic Development.
- **Preparing decisions and information for the City Council.** Staff prepare agenda materials, Council Information Memoranda, reports, ordinances, resolutions, and follow-up work for Council meetings.
- **Responding to city operations and resident needs.** Activities include permits, inspections, development applications, street and utility issues, recreation programs, police and fire calls, resident questions, and longer-term projects.

These activities suggest that information processing, communication, coordination, and documentation are important parts of staff work. Their actual time requirements should be established through interviews.
