The map

Every node and tie below is drawn from the background brief. Hover or tap any node to isolate its ties. Use the filters to drop a cluster out of view.

Reading the map PsychArmor sits at the center. Governance runs upward through the board and its Technology Committee. The external ecosystem runs outward: government and mission partners to the left, paying clients and technology vendors to the right, learners at the bottom. Our team enters at the top left.

What the map shows

  1. One way in. Alison Wagner is our only tie into the organization. Every other connection we have runs through her introduction. If she disengages, the project loses its sponsor before intake is complete.
  2. A single decision gate for technology. The path runs board, then Technology Committee, then Etienne de Bruin. Atherall's question about supplying working power routes us through that gate rather than through staff. Winning de Bruin is the difference between a project that lands and one that runs parallel to the real effort.
  3. The board is the organization's reach. Steve Schwab leads the Elizabeth Dole Foundation and Terry Kraft leads the USS Midway Museum, both already tied to PsychArmor as partner or funder. Other directors sit inside RTX, BNY Mellon, Ascential, ClearForce, Enterhealth, and Frontwave. For an organization of roughly 16 staff, the board is a distribution and revenue channel, not just a governing body.
  4. Revenue concentrates on the right side. The Veteran Ready organizations and custom-learning clients account for 90.8 percent of FY2024 revenue. Philanthropic ties on the left carry the mission but no longer carry the budget.
  5. The technology cluster has no internal ties. Five systems each connect to PsychArmor and to nothing else. That empty space is the data-fragmentation problem stated visually: learner records, donor transactions, and marketing contacts never meet.
